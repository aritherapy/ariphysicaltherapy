Conditions We Treat

Physical therapy helps with far more than most people realize. Find what you're dealing with below, or call us and we'll tell you honestly whether we can help.

Request an Evaluation

Many people don't know that physical therapists treat conditions like vertigo, bladder leaks, jaw pain and headaches, alongside back, neck and joint pain. At ARI Physical Therapy in Bakersfield, every visit is one-on-one with a licensed physical therapist, and in California you can start without a referral.

Back & Neck

Sciatica and back pain

Pain that runs from your low back into your buttock or leg, often from an irritated disc or nerve, or stiffness and aching in the back itself. Most people improve without surgery, and specific exercise and hands-on care are the recommended first step.

Read the full guide →

Headaches that start in the neck

Headaches that begin at the base of the skull or on one side of the head, often worse with certain neck positions or long hours at a desk. Hands-on treatment for the neck and targeted strengthening can reduce how often they come and how bad they get. A sudden, severe or “worst ever” headache needs a doctor right away.

Read the full guide →

Neck pain and whiplash after a car accident

Neck pain, stiffness and headaches after a car accident can show up hours or days later. Once a doctor has ruled out a serious injury, gentle movement, hands-on care and progressive exercise help you recover and get back to normal activity. If you have severe neck pain, numbness or weakness after a crash, get checked by a doctor first.

Read the full guide →

Shoulder, Elbow & Hand

Frozen shoulder and rotator cuff pain

Pain reaching overhead or behind your back, pain lying on that side at night, or a shoulder that has slowly stiffened. Physical therapy restores motion first, then rebuilds strength, and most rotator cuff and frozen shoulder problems improve without surgery.

Read the full guide →

Tennis elbow and golfer’s elbow

Pain on the outside (tennis) or inside (golfer’s) of the elbow, worse with gripping, lifting or typing, and you don't have to play either sport to get it. Progressive tendon-strengthening exercise, changes to how you grip and work, and hands-on care are the mainstays of treatment.

Read the full guide →

Carpal tunnel and hand numbness

Numbness or tingling in the thumb, index and middle fingers, often worse at night, driving or typing. For mild to moderate symptoms, physical therapy with nerve and tendon gliding exercises, splinting advice and changes to how you use your hands can help. Constant numbness or hand weakness should be checked by a doctor.

Read the full guide →

Hip, Knee & Foot

Knee arthritis and knee pain

Pain on stairs, stiffness after sitting, or a knee that swells after a busy day. Exercise-based physical therapy is one of the most strongly recommended treatments for knee osteoarthritis, and for some people it can put off the need for surgery.

Read the full guide →

Plantar fasciitis and heel pain

Sharp pain in the bottom of your heel with your first steps in the morning or after sitting. Hands-on therapy, stretching, taping, strengthening and footwear advice treat it without injections or surgery for most people.

Read the full guide →

Women’s Pelvic Health

Pelvic floor therapy at ARI is for women, always one-on-one in a private room.

Bladder leaks (urinary incontinence)

Leaking when you cough, sneeze, laugh, lift or exercise, or sudden urges you can't hold. Supervised pelvic floor muscle training is recommended as a first treatment for stress and mixed incontinence in women, before medication or surgery.

Read the full guide →

Pelvic organ prolapse

A feeling of heaviness, pressure or bulging in the vagina, often after childbirth or menopause and worse at the end of the day. Supervised pelvic floor muscle training is recommended for symptoms of mild to moderate prolapse, along with lifting and bowel habits that reduce strain.

Read the full guide →

Pelvic pain and painful sex

Pain with intercourse, pelvic or tailbone pain, or pain with tampons or exams is often linked to pelvic floor muscles that are tight or overworked. Pelvic floor therapy helps these muscles relax and work properly, gently and at your pace, in a private room.

Read the full guide →

Diastasis recti (separated abdominal muscles)

A gap or bulge down the middle of your belly after pregnancy, sometimes with low back pain or a “pooch” that exercise hasn't fixed. Physical therapy retrains your deep core and pelvic floor to work together so you can return to exercise with confidence.

Read the full guide →

Dizziness, Jaw & Balance

Vertigo and dizziness (BPPV)

The room spins for a few seconds when you roll over in bed, lie down or look up. This is often BPPV, the most common cause of vertigo, and repositioning treatment by a trained physical therapist often helps within a few visits.

Read the full guide →

TMJ and jaw pain

Aching in the jaw or temples, painful clicking, trouble opening wide, or clenching and grinding. Gentle hands-on care, jaw and neck exercises and habit changes are an effective first treatment for most TMJ problems.

Read the full guide →

Bone Health & Surgery

Osteoporosis and bone health

If you've been told you have osteopenia or osteoporosis, the right exercise matters. Physical therapy teaches safe strengthening and weight-bearing exercise, balance training to lower your risk of falls, and ways to move that protect your spine.

Read the full guide →

Getting stronger before surgery (prehab)

Building strength and motion before a planned knee, hip or shoulder surgery, learning the exercises you'll use afterward, and preparing your home and routine, so recovery starts on a better footing.

Read the full guide →

Recovery after surgery

Rehabilitation after joint replacement, ACL and rotator cuff repair, spine surgery and more, coordinated with your surgeon's protocol.

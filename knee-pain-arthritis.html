Knee Arthritis and Knee Pain Treatment in Bakersfield

Arthritis doesn't have to mean giving up walks, stairs or the things you enjoy. Exercise-based physical therapy is one of the most strongly recommended treatments for knee osteoarthritis.

Request an Evaluation

Yes, physical therapy helps knee arthritis. The American College of Rheumatology and the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons both strongly recommend exercise and physical therapy for knee osteoarthritis, to reduce pain and improve how well you move. For some people it can put off the need for surgery, and if you do eventually need a knee replacement, going in stronger helps your recovery. At ARI Physical Therapy in Bakersfield, every visit is one-on-one with a licensed physical therapist.

Does this sound like you?

✓Stairs hurt, especially going down
✓Getting up from a chair or out of the car takes effort
✓Your knee is stiff in the morning or after sitting
✓It swells or aches after a busy day or a long walk
✓Grinding or crackling, or the knee feels like it might give way
✓You've started avoiding walks, kneeling or activities you love

Why it happens, and why exercise helps

Osteoarthritis is a gradual change in the cartilage and bone of the joint. But pain isn't only about cartilage: weak thigh and hip muscles, stiffness, extra body weight and an old injury all affect how much load the knee takes. That's why what shows on an X-ray often doesn't match how much it hurts, and why making the muscles around the knee stronger can reduce pain even though the X-ray doesn't change.

Movement is good for arthritic joints. The goal is the right amount of the right exercise, not avoiding activity.

What treatment at ARI looks like

1
A one-on-one evaluation

Your first visit takes about 45 to 60 minutes. We check your knee and hip strength, motion, balance and walking, and ask which daily tasks matter most to you: stairs, walks, gardening, keeping up with grandkids.

2
Strength and movement

Progressive strengthening for the thigh and hip muscles, hands-on treatment to improve stiffness, and balance work, adjusted so the knee is challenged without flaring up.

3
Real-life skills

Easier ways to handle stairs, chairs and the car, a walking plan you can build on, advice on a brace or cane if it would help, and a home program to keep the gains going.

No referral needed to start. In California, you can see a physical therapist directly for up to 12 visits or 45 days, whichever comes first. Some insurance plans still require a referral for coverage; call us at (661) 282-8584 and we'll help you check.

When to see a doctor first

See a doctor promptly if you have:

  • A hot, red, swollen knee, especially with a fever
  • Sudden, severe swelling, or a knee injury where you can't put weight on the leg
  • A knee that locks and won't straighten
  • Calf pain, swelling or warmth in your lower leg, which needs urgent attention to rule out a blood clot
